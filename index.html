    About

    A sushi chef learning his cuts, except the fish is a language model. I take them apart by hand to ask an old question with new tools: is anything in there awake, and how would we know? I work at the intersection of mechanistic interpretability, learning theory, philosophy of mind, and the science of consciousness. I am currently a Research Collaborator at SDU’s Data and Intelligence Lab, supervised by Serkan Ayvaz, and at the HKUST DataVISards group. I am an incoming MPhil student in Machine Learning and Machine Intelligence at the University of Cambridge.

    My research is driven by one conviction: whether AI systems can be conscious is an empirical question, and answering it requires rigorous, testable theories grounded in how models actually work internally. The work is organised around two questions:

    1. How does optimization and learning give rise to the structures consciousness requires, and can we trace them mechanistically inside real models? This connects learning theory and mechanistic interpretability: asking what gradient descent inevitably builds, whether self-models and introspective representations emerge as byproducts of prediction, and whether those structures are detectable at the circuit level and traceable inside real models.

    2. What are the necessary and sufficient computational conditions for machine consciousness, and do current AI systems satisfy them? This connects philosophy of mind and consciousness science: taking Integrated Information Theory, Global Workspace Theory, and Active Inference seriously enough to generate distinct, falsifiable predictions, then testing them on real models.
