Every Mirror Has a Blind Spot: Fixed-Point Limits on Machine Introspection

Headline result

Perfect self-prediction is equivalent to a fixed-point condition on the system's response function. For any specific system, the structural self-prediction gap is a deterministic, assumption-free quantity. Under marginally uniform response functions its expectation is ((n−1)/n)^n ≥ 1/4, rising to 1/e ≈ 37% as the output space grows, with a distribution-free sensitivity bound that holds whenever no single output is too often self-consistent. Lifting to distributional self-models restores existence via Brouwer but makes fixed-point computation PPAD-hard. On Gemma-2-2B with |O|=4, 7 of 10 mapped response functions are outright fixed-point-free, well above the uniform baseline (3/4)^4 ≈ 0.32.

Method in brief

Self-modeling systems are formalised as triples (M, M̂, f) and perfect self-prediction is shown to be equivalent to a fixed-point condition on the system's response function. The structural gap is characterised by two complementary bounds: a sharp expected value ((n−1)/n)^n under marginal uniformity, and a distribution-free sensitivity bound using only linearity of expectation and the diagonal probabilities p_o = Pr[g_x(o)=o]. For continuous distributional self-models, Brouwer's theorem guarantees fixed-point existence but computation is PPAD-hard. In any action-mediated environment the self-model is shown to be the information-theoretic bottleneck for future observations. The fixed-point obstruction is read as the Lawvere categorical dual of Gödel's first incompleteness theorem. The framework is validated with Monte Carlo experiments on random response functions, a toy MLP self-modeling system, and an empirical probe on Gemma-2-2B.

Abstract

How accurately can a system predict its own outputs? We formalise self-modeling systems as triples (M, M̂, f) and show that perfect self-prediction is equivalent to the self-model landing on a fixed point of a system-defined response function. For any specific system, the structural self-prediction gap is then a deterministic, assumption-free quantity: the measure of inputs where no such fixed point exists. Averaging this quantity under marginally uniform response functions yields a lower bound of ((n−1)/n)^n ≥ 1/4, and a distribution-free sensitivity bound holds whenever no single output is too often self-consistent. Lifting self-models from point predictions to distributions restores fixed-point existence via Brouwer, but computing the fixed points is PPAD-hard: the structural barrier does not vanish, it becomes computational. Self-prediction also cannot be side-stepped. In any environment that reacts to a system only through its actions, every bit of information the internal state carries about future observations is already captured by predicting the next action, making the self-model the information-theoretic bottleneck. Chained together, these results imply that every prediction-dependent self-modeling system faces an irreducible gap, structural or computational, that no architecture, training procedure, or capacity can close. An empirical probe on Gemma-2-2B exhibits the predicted pattern. We read the fixed-point obstruction as the Lawvere categorical dual of Gödel's first incompleteness theorem, inverting the Penrose–Lucas argument: the same self-referential structure Penrose invoked against machine consciousness is the universal mathematical room in which any self-modeling theory of mind must live.
