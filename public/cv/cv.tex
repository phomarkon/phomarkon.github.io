\section*{Summary}
I study how and why models learn, how what they learn is stored and represented, and how we get to models we can actually trust, with mechanistic interpretability as one lens among several. I run the full research lifecycle myself: problem formulation, experimental design, pipeline engineering, manuscript writing, and venue selection. Current work spans circuit-level audits of routing behaviour installed by fine-tuning (under review, NeurIPS 2026), evidential standards for safety claims, causal tracing of moral reasoning, and a cross-family study of chain-of-thought safety, alongside work on vision, medical-imaging, and remote-sensing models. The questions, not any one model family, are what I am after.

My conviction is simple: we do not need smarter models, we need more aligned ones. My goal from here is a PhD. I do not yet know which of my questions is the one I will spend years on, and I am comfortable with that for now, because I am following the curiosity and the questions keep getting sharper. I am a final-year BSc Software Engineering student at the University of Southern Denmark, admitted for 2026 to the MPhil in Machine Learning and Machine Intelligence at the University of Cambridge and the MSc in Machine Learning at the University of T\"ubingen, and I bring an unconventional background (four years of military service, three startups, and a semester at HKUST) that shapes how I identify problems worth solving.

\section*{Selected Publications}

2026 \quad \textbf{P.~M.~Konrad}, T.~Tanyel, and S.~Ayvaz. Routing Subspaces: Auditing Evaluation-to-Deployment Mismatch in Fine-Tuned Language Models. Under review, \textit{NeurIPS 2026}. \href{https://phomarkon.github.io/papers/NeurIPS_2026_Routing_Subspaces.pdf}{[PDF]}\\
\textsf{TL;DR.} Path-patching localises an eval-vs-deployment signal to a narrow mid-depth attention window. Clamping a low-dimensional subspace closes the gap in 11 of 12 cells.

2026 \quad \textbf{P.~M.~Konrad}, T.~Tanyel, and S.~Ayvaz. Acceptance Cards: A Four-Diagnostic Standard for Safe Fine-Tuning Defense Claims. Under review, \textit{NeurIPS 2026}. \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10575}{arXiv:2605.10575}\\
\textsf{TL;DR.} A four-gate evidential standard for safe-fine-tuning defense claims. SafeLoRA fails the full-card pass under matched-action audit.

2026 \quad \textbf{P.~M.~Konrad}, T.~Tanyel, and S.~Ayvaz. Decoded but Unused: Instruction Tuning Routes Moral Framing into the Judgment Readout. Under review, \textit{ICML 2026 Workshop on Mechanistic Interpretability}. \href{https://phomarkon.github.io/papers/ICML_2026_Decoded_but_Unused.pdf}{[PDF]}\\
\textsf{TL;DR.} Moral framing is linearly decodable in pretrained models but only causally routed to judgment after instruction tuning.

2026 \quad \textbf{P.~M.~Konrad} and S.~Ayvaz. When does chain-of-thought improve safety? Evidence from 18 models across 5 families. Under review, \textit{COLM 2026}. \href{https://phomarkon.github.io/papers/COLM_2026_When_Does_Chain_of_Thought_Improve_Safety.pdf}{[PDF]}\\
\textsf{TL;DR.} CoT does not automatically improve safety calibration. Training objective (GRPO) matters more than deliberation itself.

2026 \quad \textbf{P.~M.~Konrad}, T.~Tanyel, and S.~Ayvaz. Self-Reports Do Not Identify Self-Models: An Identifiability Test for Counterfactual Reports. Accepted (oral), \textit{PhilML Workshop, ICML 2026}. \href{https://phomarkon.github.io/papers/ICML_2026_Workshop_Counterfactual_Self_Reports_Not_Well_Posed.pdf}{[PDF]}\\
\textsf{TL;DR.} Self-reports are evidence about behavior in a prompt, not proof of a self-model. Wrong-source demonstrations pull affect-state reports toward the source family unless mechanism binding holds.
