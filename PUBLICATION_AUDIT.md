# Publication audit

Audit date: 21 July 2026

## Method and limits

This audit covers every scholarly PDF in the repository and cross-checks titles, author order, venue/status wording, links, and available contribution statements against the editable CV, site data, OpenReview, arXiv links, and proceedings records. Full local manuscripts were text-extracted and reviewed, including methods, results, limitations, conclusions, reproducibility sections, and CRediT statements where present.

The Google Scholar profile returned HTTP 403 to automated access. Citation counts are therefore deliberately not used. “First author” records author position, not sole ownership. Where a paper has no author-contribution statement, individual contribution is marked as undocumented rather than inferred.

## Selected-work decision

The homepage should feature four works:

1. **Decoded but Unused**: best combination of current alignment, controlled causal intervention, technical depth, and reproducibility.
2. **Self-Reports Do Not Identify Self-Models**: strongest direct evidence for the identifiability and evidence-auditing question, with oral workshop validation.
3. **Coverage Debt**: clearest documented individual ownership and strongest end-to-end systems evidence, while remaining explicit that it is a preprint and no longer the main direction.
4. **Beyond Major Floods**: a real peer-reviewed proceedings paper and compact evidence that a complete applied ML pipeline was finished and published.

The medical segmentation paper ranks highly on empirical depth and verified contribution, but it is omitted from the homepage because four slots are already filled and its current status is only a preprint. The fruit study has strong ownership and careful confound control, but is newer, preliminary, and lacks a public code release. Acceptance Cards is relevant but still a submitted preprint with a narrow single-family audit. The network-neuroscience, software-architecture, retrieval, and governance papers are legitimate outputs but are less useful as first-pass evidence of the current trajectory.

## Maintenance and supersession

- Coverage Debt explicitly supersedes and corrects parts of the single-zone BSc-thesis analysis; the thesis remains a degree artifact, while the preprint is the research output to cite.
- The KES paper is the final published version and should not be duplicated as a preprint.
- The three ICSA workshop papers should be listed once each with their exact workshop venue and arXiv link.
- The two ICML workshop papers with OpenReview pages should prefer OpenReview while retaining local archival PDFs.
- Acceptance Cards and The Open-Box Fallacy must be described as preprints, regardless of the `NeurIPS_2026` local filename.
- No evidence in the repository establishes that any listed work is abandoned. Local PDFs are current as of the audit, but promised or anonymous code links should be replaced with permanent public repositories when available.
